What Is Hockey?

Hockey is a fast-paced game played on ice. Players skate across the rink and attempt to put a rubber disk, called a puck, into the opposing team's net.

Positions and Roles
Position Role
GoalieProtects the net and prevents the puck from crossing the goal line using specialized pads and gloves.
DefensemanDefends the defensive zone, clears rebounds, and starts offensive breakouts with transition passes.
Left WingGenerates offensive pressure along the left boards, battles in the corners, and covers opposing defensemen.
CenterTakes faceoffs, quarterbacks offensive rushes, and covers both ends of the ice.
Right WingCarries the puck down the right wing, forechecks opposing defenders, and drives scoring opportunities.

When

While hockey can be played year-round at indoor arenas, organized leagues follow specific calendars ranging from winter games to off-season conditioning.

When Is Hockey Played?

Hockey is typically played during the fall, winter, and early spring months.

What Does a Hockey Schedule Look Like?

Recreational and collegiate hockey schedules often include two to three practices per week, one or two games a week, and occasional tournaments or playoffs.

When Do Games Take Place?

Games typically take place on Friday, Saturday, or Sunday nights. Tournaments may include different time slots.

Where

Hockey is especially popular in northern states of the United States, Canada, and parts of Europe. The sport has a strong identity around the world.

Notable Hockey Hotbeds

Minnesota

Minnesota is known as the State of Hockey and has produced many NHL and NCAA Division I hockey players.

Canada

Canada has a long hockey tradition, including outdoor hockey rinks built on ponds and lakes.

Russia

Russia has an established hockey culture and a major professional league, the KHL.

How

Starting hockey is a lengthy and unusual process compared to most sports. It requires lots of gear and multiple practices in order to be coordinated enough to play.

Equipment Needed to Start Playing Hockey
EquipmentPurpose
HelmetProtects the head and face from pucks, sticks, and falls.
Hockey StickUsed for stickhandling, passing, and shooting the puck.
Protective PadsShin, elbow, shoulder, and other pads help protect against impacts and falls.
Hockey SkatesProvide ankle support and steel blades for moving on the ice.
GlovesProtect the hands and help players grip their sticks.
Hockey PantsProvide protection for the hips and upper legs.

Your Hockey Development Ladder

After getting the necessary equipment, work through these three stages to move from learning basic skating to playing organized hockey.

1. Public Skate

Start by learning the basics of skating during public sessions. Practice balance, stopping, turning, and moving comfortably on the ice.

→

2. Stick and Puck

Once you can skate confidently, attend stick-and-puck sessions to practice stickhandling, passing, and shooting alongside other players.

→

3. Recreational League

Join a recreational league to use your skills in games, learn teamwork, and gain experience playing with a team.

Why

Reasons to Play Hockey

  • Lifelong Friendships: Hockey helps you build close friendships with teammates and become part of a community that can stay with you for life.
  • Better Cardiovascular Health: Skating and playing hockey can improve cardiovascular health.
  • Stay in Shape: Hockey is a fun way to exercise, stay active, and build endurance.

Reasons Not to Play Hockey

  • High Cost: Equipment and team fees can be expensive.
  • Difficult to Learn: Skating, passing, and shooting take time and practice.
  • Limited Rink Access: Some people live far from an ice rink.
